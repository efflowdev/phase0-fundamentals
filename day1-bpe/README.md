# Day 1 — Byte Pair Encoding (BPE)

This day focused on building a small byte-pair encoding pipeline from scratch and comparing it to tiktoken. The implementation trains a set of merge rules from a corpus, encodes text into token IDs, and decodes those IDs back to the original string.

Three measurements. **Compression vs. tiktoken:** on the same ~4.4-4.5K character Lorem Ipsum passage, tiktoken's `cl100k_base` used 1039 tokens for the English version, 1361 for a Romanian translation, and 1621 for Russian — about 0.24, 0.31, and 0.36 tokens per character respectively. Russian costs roughly 1.5x English for the same amount of text. **Word splitting:** `"strawberry"` becomes 3 tokens in tiktoken (`str`/`aw`/`berry`) vs. 8 in mine (`st`/`r`/`a`/`w`/`b`/`er`/`r`/`y`), several of them single letters. **Round-trip:** `decode(encode(x)) == x` passes on the training corpus and on a string mixing accented Latin, Cyrillic, Japanese, and emoji.

The surprise: the `"strawberry"` split isn't a fixed property of the word — it's an artifact of the training corpus. My tokenizer never saw "berry" in ~4.4K characters of Lorem Ipsum, so it fell back to near-letter-level fragments; tiktoken's internet-scale vocabulary had seen it often enough to keep it mostly whole. That's also *why* Russian costs more: a tokenizer's vocabulary is shaped by what it was trained on, and anything far from that distribution — a different script, a rare word — pays for it in token count.
